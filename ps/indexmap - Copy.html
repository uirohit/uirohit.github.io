Intro

Project Overview

I worked on the trading software used for NSE/BSE and MCX workflows, focusing on understanding how professional traders monitor strategies, manage orders, evaluate positions, and make rapid execution decisions.

The challenge wasn't to redesign the platform from scratch.

The challenge was to improve the experience without disrupting the workflows traders already knew.

Because traders were already familiar with the existing software and its shortcuts, controls, and interaction patterns, any major change could introduce unnecessary learning time and affect their execution speed.

Business Goals

  • Providing high freqency/low latency trading to retail traders
  • Reduce the cost to 40% what the competitors is charginig right now
  • Providing right training for retail traders about HFT

Defining the project

What I was designing

The project covered two trading environments — NSE/BSE and MCX — used by traders to manage strategies, execute orders and monitor live positions.

The goal was to improve the existing experience while keeping the core workflows, terminology and shortcuts familiar to existing traders.

Key Constraints

  • • Existing traders were already comfortable with the current software.
  • • Major workflow changes would require additional training.
  • • Trading decisions happen under high time pressure.
  • • The interface needs to handle large amounts of real-time data.
  • • Keyboard shortcuts and fast interactions were essential to the workflow.

Design Focus

Rather than redesigning everything, I focused on finding specific areas where the experience could be improved without disrupting established trading behaviour.

Understand option trading

Understanding Options Trading

Before working on the trading interface, I first built a basic understanding of options trading and the terminology used by traders.

I studied how options work, the difference between Call and Put options, and how traders combine multiple legs to create different strategies.

I also looked into:

  • • Call & Put options
  • • Option ratios
  • • Multi-leg strategies
  • • Call-Put combinations
  • • Bullish, Bearish, Directional & Neutral strategies
  • • How traders create and manage these strategies

This helped me understand the logic behind the screens I was designing — not just what each field or number represented, but why a trader needed it while building and managing a strategy.

Understand competitors softwares

Learning the Trading Environment

Before designing the product, I needed to understand how professional traders actually work.

I spent time learning and using competing trading platforms to understand their workflows, controls, keyboard shortcuts, and information hierarchy. This helped me look at the product from a trader’s perspective rather than simply from a designer’s perspective.

Competitors studied

Cogmac · UTrade · Share India

What I studied

  • • Trading dashboards and market data
  • • Strategy creation and multi-leg workflows
  • • Buy / Sell and order execution
  • • Rejected orders, positions and MTM
  • • Strategy monitoring and controls
  • • Keyboard-driven interactions

I then mapped the complete trading workflow:

Create Strategy → Configure Legs → Monitor Market → Place Orders → Manage Positions → Track MTM → Modify / Pause / Resume

Introduced New Feature

Proposed Feature — Average Rate (AR) & Average Rate Touch (ART)

AR — Average Rate

AR tracks the price at which the market repeatedly reverses against the order, while ART records how many times that reversal has occurred.

ART — Average Rate Touch

ART tracks the number of times the market reaches the Average Rate and reverses, helping traders identify how consistently that price level is being tested.

This gives traders a quick reference point to understand where repeated reversals are occurring and use that data to support their trading decisions.
