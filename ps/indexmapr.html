NSE/BSE & MCX Trading Workflows

Mapr is a high-performance trading platform built specifically for Indian financial markets (NSE/BSE and MCX). It enables active traders to monitor live market indicators, build option strategies, handle open orders, and evaluate running positions with sub-millisecond execution speeds.

The Challenge: Traders are heavily dependent on keyboard shortcuts and dense terminal grids they've memorized over years. The primary objective was to modernize the interface and make the information architecture cleaner, without slowing down their muscle memory and workflow speed.

Learning to use the computer software

In the first phase of our research, we observed professional traders. They don't just use one screen — they have complex, high-density multi-monitor setups to track distinct market vectors simultaneously.

To understand their rapid execution workflow, we mapped and analyzed their primary screen views:

  • • Scanners: Real-time price feed tables containing volume spikes, volatility breakouts, and delta alerts to detect immediate trade opportunities.
  • • All in Indices: A macro tracking screen providing global and sectoral indices dashboards to judge market sentiment.
  • • OI (Open Interest): Visual representation of volume trends to identify build-ups, short coverings, and long unwinding strikes.

Proposed New Feature: AR & ART

AR (Average Rate) and ART (Average Rate Trend tracker). AR tracks the rate at which the market options setup reverses and shows how many times that reversal has occurred (ARC - Average Rate Count) to measure momentum strength.

Algorithm Log Trace

ar_art_algorithm.log
> INIT ORDER: SELL ORDER [SIZE = 10]
> READING STRIKE OPTIONS... AT 45,400
> DETECTION: Market moves = 45.40 = REVERSAL [01]
> DETECTION: Market moves = 45.40 = REVERSAL [02]
> DETECTION: Market moves = 45.40 = REVERSAL [03]
> CALCULATING: AR = 45.40 | ARC = 3

By reading continuous option chain shifts (call vs put writing thresholds) under active sell orders, the system measures reversal instances. This lets traders dynamically recalibrate order sizes and stop-losses.
